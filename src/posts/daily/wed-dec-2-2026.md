### Notes

(See Saturday for authors.)	

*In Psalm 138 King David, speaking by the Spirit of Christ, declares his prayers and praises and his covenantal confidence that God will fulfill all the promises of 2 Samuel 7 and Psalm 2. He walks through troubles and has many enemies, but God will keep him, establish him, and give him victory. In his words we hear the voice of Jesus in his earthly life as he appropriates by faith these words for himself. And now in Christ we join with him in saying them as his church.*

*In Psalm 138:3 the King celebrates the stupendous fact that the covenant God answered his prayer on the day [he] called. Foundationally, this relationship is that between the Father and the eternal Son, in which the Son can say, “I knew that you always hear me” (John 11:42). The prayers of David were heard on the basis of the atoning death of Jesus Christ, as were all prayers before Christ and since, to the end of time. … Nothing so invigorates the spirit as answered prayer.*

Christopher Ash, Psalms 101–150, vol. 4, The Psalms: A Christ-Centered Commentary.
