### Notes

(See Saturday for authors.)	

A common objection to Christianity is that "It's been 2,000 years since Jesus promised to return. Isn't that a bit too long?" If you are honest, you must deal with this question and perhaps admit that you may have asked it yourself.

Interestingly, we find in Scriptures that both the prophets of the Old Testament and the apostles of the New have had to deal with similar questions (Jer. 17: 15–18; Amos 5: 18–20; Rom. 8: 18–25). Especially the prophets tell us that the day of the Lord is a day of terrible judgment. It is often described in Scriptures as a day of destruction and calamity, where the Lord will melt creation down to the very elements and take revenge on his enemies. However terrifying the scene will certainly be, it is very comforting to know that God will finally judge all evil and establish justice forever on earth: no more corruption, bribery, oppression, murder.

However, the apostles also remind us that God chose a people to save and preserve from the final judgment. The Eternal will delay his judgment until all his children come to repentance. The set time for his judgment remains a mystery, but the time for repentance is today\!
