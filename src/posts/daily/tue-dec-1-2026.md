### Notes

(See Saturday for authors.)	

Once we think of ourselves rightly, we can think and act rightly toward others. Some of these instructions are for how we act toward other believers; others are toward other people in general.

God gives us each faith, and He gives us each gifts. These gifts are for the church, not for our own selfish uses. As we serve the church with our gifts, we show our love to be genuine.

That love is to look like (and be) deep family love. Calling one another in the church brother and sister is good. Showing brotherly and sisterly love is even better. 

That showing love includes honoring others, giving to their needs as we can, having others in our homes. It involves reacting rightly toward others—blessing them even if they hurt us, sharing in their joys and sorrows, and just plain getting along. It includes being humble enough to be with people in even more humble circumstances than ours. 

Finally, it involves being a peacemaker—such a peacemaker that we’ll do everything that we can do to be at peace. Everything can’t, of course, involve sinful compromise or sinful actions. Short of that, though, do all you can do to make and keep peace with others. Since we are seeking peace, we can leave any vengeance with God.
